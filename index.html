Kendall Duffey's Tips: How to Conduct a Good Interview

An Instruction Challenge for JOUR108

How to interview someone for an article/story ...

Assembly time: 1 hour
Awesome rating: 100% awesome

Need to interview someone for Jour 107, or because you're writing an article, but don't know where to start? Follow these steps to come up with questions and ensure that you gather all the information needed to hold an effective interview.

  • Things You'll Need

  • Something to take notes (iPad, notepad, etc)
  • Pencil or pen
  • A creative mind
  • Interviewee

  1. Research the person.

    Before writing questions, gather all the information you can. Know about significant accomplishments, biographical information, failures, and overall personality. Try searching online for articles about them to find out more.

  2. Write down your goal for the interview.

    This can help you create questions before the interview that can lead it in the right direction. This will also allow you to stay on track if the conversation goes in a different direction. Your objective should be a concise declarative statement.

    Knowing what you want to get out of the interview can be very beneficial.

  3. Lead with a “softball question”.

    An easy starting question will make the interviewee become relaxed and open up. This type of question should be simple and not challenging.

  4. Ask open-ended questions.

    Create a dialogue, and gain as much knowledge as you can. Try avoiding simple “yes” or “no” questions, as the interviewee may not give descriptive answers. Asking this person what they enjoyed or disliked about the subject you’re interviewing for will give you insightful information.

  5. Ask a reflection question.

    Learn how this person thinks and what they value. Questions that make your interviewee share a story/example keep things going and give you useful information. When creating questions, try to find any information on your interviewees career path.

  6. Write down questions you know the answer to.

    Write down questions you want answered, and that you know the answer to. Then answer these questions before your interview. If you know the answer to certain questions, you might not need to ask them during an interview.

  7. Ask questions that cause an emotional response.

    Just like open ended questions, you’ll want to come up with a few that will get you an emotional response. When writing questions, research if your subject has anything from the past that may elicit a feeling-based response. If there’s nothing, come up with a question on the spot during the interview. Use what’s been discussed in the interview and write down this new question quickly so you don’t forget. Make sure to ask “why” and “how”.

  8. Include a curve-ball.

    If questions get too repetitive, get creative and ask something else. The question doesn’t have to be an attacking question, it can be a fun unrelated question.

  9. Rephrase your questions.

    Look over all of your questions and rephrase ones that still need a little work, or that don’t help you reach your objective. Let the flow of the conversation help you ask your questions. Use the ones you’ve written as much as you can, but be prepared to ignore the irrelevant ones.
